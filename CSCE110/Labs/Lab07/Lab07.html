Q1: Queue

Think of a coffee shop queue. The person who is first in line is served first. People enter the queue at the back and leave the queue when they are served. In computing, we frequently need to simulate systems like this. Python lists can simulate queues with the help of append and pop functions. The append function takes a value and adds it to the end of the list. The pop function takes an index and removes the value at that index from the queue. It also shifts the list so that there are no empty spaces in the list.  Write a function, list_as_queue() (in Lab07.py) that simulates the coffee shop queue. We are keeping it simple so that 5 customers come in before service begins and no customers come in after service begins. You need to serve the customers on a first come first serve basis. Do not use loops. 

Example #1. The names in the first 5 lines are user input. The output begins on the very next line.

list_as_queue()

Welcome to our Coffee Shop. What is your name : Suleman
Welcome to our Coffee Shop. What is your name : Jake
Welcome to our Coffee Shop. What is your name : Dylan
Welcome to our Coffee Shop. What is your name : Ethan
Welcome to our Coffee Shop. What is your name : Mark
Here is your coffee Suleman. Have a nice day.
Here is your coffee Jake. Have a nice day.
Here is your coffee Dylan. Have a nice day.
Here is your coffee Ethan. Have a nice day.
Here is your coffee Mark. Have a nice day.

 

Q2: Stack

Another common method to organize data is the stack. Here, think of a stack of homework assignments. The assignment that is submitted last is on top of the pile. It is graded first by the TA’s because it is on top. List append and pop functions give you a way to simulate a stack as well. Write a function, list_as_stack() (in Lab07.py) to model the assignment stack. Suppose we have a total of 4 students. Prompt them for names as they submit assignments. Then grade the assignments from the stack and assign them scores based on the ASCII equivalent of the first character of their names.

Example #1. The output begins on the very next line.

list_as_stack()

Thanks for submitting. What is your name: Dylan
Thanks for submitting. What is your name: Mark
Thanks for submitting. What is your name: Ethan
Thanks for submitting. What is your name: Jake
Jake, your score is: 74
Ethan, your score is: 69
Mark, your score is: 77
Dylan, your score is: 68

Note: In the last two questions. You should be annoyed at the level of repetition. We will get rid of that in a couple of weeks.

 

Q3: List Methods

Write a function, list_test (in Lab07.py) that initializes a list of 4 integers and prints the list in ascending order and descending order. You will find the list.sort function helpful for it. You need to prompt the user for 4 numbers.

Example #1.

list_test()

1st : 2
2nd : 7
3rd : 5
4th : 1
Ascending Order : [1, 2, 5, 7]
Descending Order : [7, 5, 2, 1]

Q4: Data Processing

A common use of strings is to process data from files one line at a time. In this problem (process_line() function in Lab07.py), the user will provide one line to the program as input, and you will output processed line as properly formatted output. The input will be a student's record, and you will be calculating the total score for the student and printing it out in the nice format shown in the example below. Later on, we can put the code you write today in a loop and process a whole list of students. Do not use conditionals or loops for this process. The number of assignments for the students will always be 4. All fields in the output must be 12 characters. All numbers must be displayed as floats with exactly 2 decimal places and all fields must be left aligned. 

Example #1. 

Enter Student's Record:
123456, Suleman, Mahmood, 5, 20, 25, 30

Formatted Result:
UIN         Last Name   First Name  Activities  Labs        Homework    Exams       Total
123456      Mahmood     Suleman     5.00        20.00       25.00       30.00       80.00

Notes:
You can str.split method to break the input on comma (','). Use list indexing to extract different parts and process them. You may need to use str.strip methods as well.

 

Q5: Dot Product

Write a function dot_product(l1, l2) which calculates dot product of two vectors of length 3. The dot product is the sum of products of corresponding elements in two vectors. For example, the dot product of vectors [1, 2, 3] and [4, 3, 2] will be (1 * 4 + 2 * 3 + 3 * 2) = 16. Return the result. Do not use loops. We can generalize this later.

Examples

dot_product([1, 2, 3], [4, 3, 2])                # evaluates to 16
dot_product([10, 20, 15], [0.1, 0.2, 0.5])       # evaluates to 12.5
